Run **Phase U** first (§0a): upgrading to engine 0.1.57 changes the cost model —
the ~#4420~ batch-slot parking fix, measured Arc prompt/parallel gains in 0.2.9,
and 0.2.10's new K/V-window ladder that may let us keep ~196608~ with four lanes
— then re-measure and decide. Phase C is the recommendation once the
orchestrator/subagent workflow is the dominant load: **four lanes at ~98k cost the same VRAM as the two lanes measured
at 196608** (4 × 98304 = 2 × 196608 = 393,216 tokens of session K/V), so it buys
double the concurrency for nothing beyond a smaller pi window. Phase B is the
fallback if the orchestrator cannot live inside 98k.

Three things gate any of this, and a fourth sets expectations:
1. ~--batch-mtp~ is *not optional* — ~--batch~ alone measured −59 % on this box (§3).
2. The native-mode wrapper in ~peteches/services/strata.scm~ silently dropped
   ~--batch~/~--parallel~ and ~extra-args~ (§6), so batching could not be switched
   on from the Guix config at all — **this is fixed** (§11), and the measured
   answer is that on this card it should stay off. The ~STRATA_BATCH_MTP=1~
   environment route is still the only part that worked without a patch.
3. Every slot's K/V is sized by the *engine-wide* ~--context~, not by what a
   request actually uses (~--max-sequence-length~ is unsupported on SYCL,
   ~--kv-grow~ auto-disables here). So context is the price of concurrency, and
   pi's compaction settings are the lever that lets us pay less.
4. Batching does **not** prefill in parallel — the server admits requests one at
   a time (§3a). What it buys is decode overlap, cheaper CPU-expert amortisation
   per row, and prompt-read yielding for the orchestrator's 65 k+ reads. Run
   §6a's free levers first; set ~--parallel~ to the usual fan-out *plus the
   orchestrator*, not to a hope.

* 1. Why the economics flip under subagents
Measured over 3,559 logged requests (1–6 Nov, engine 0.1.52, batch off):

- 32.9 requests/hour, 207–1157 per day, mean duration 31.4 s, mean output 1148 tokens.
- **Time-weighted mean concurrency 0.30, peak 2.** Only **1 of 60 traffic hours**
  exceeded one stream's capacity (3,663 s busy vs 3,600), and only **3.3 % of all
  busy time** sits in that hour.
- Prompt-dominated agent loops: 98.7 % prefix reuse (98.7 M of 113 M tokens read),
  working prompts up to 146,264 tokens, TTFT p95 far worse than decode:
  observed 33.2 s, 46.3 s and **120.2 s** waiting behind one long read/decode.
- Single-stream ceiling ~**60 tok/s** (MTP drafts accepted ~65 %), and it is
  flat: 1, 2 and 4 open connections all measured ~52–61 tok/s aggregate.

So batch=1 was chosen correctly for the traffic of the last months — the engine
was idle ~74 % of the time and batching had nothing to buy. The orchestrator +
subagents workflow changes the shape in two ways at once:

- N lanes arrive *simultaneously*, each with a small, independent prompt
  (the bundled ~scout.md~/~researcher.md~/~worker.md~ definitions all say
  "operate in an isolated context … all necessary context is in the task
  description"). That is exactly the load 2–4 slots serve.
- The orchestrator itself stops needing a huge window, because its subagents
  return *summaries*, not raw context. Its 113–146 k prompts were a symptom of
  doing everything in one context.

Both effects point the same way: smaller ~--context~, more lanes.

Two consequences that matter more than anything else:

1. **The cache ceiling is ~18,300 slots regardless of context.** Raising
   ~--parallel~ at a small context stops paying for itself once ~N × context ×
   9.3 kB~ pushes the cache back to that ceiling — which happens around
   ~--context 163840~ at 1 lane. Below that, every GiB freed by a smaller window
   can go to experts.
2. **Batch windows on this card are half CPU.** With the ~xe~ driver
   ~--pcie-frac~ is pinned at 0 ("the GPU faults on pageable host memory, so
   --pcie-frac defaults to 0 here (the CPU computes the experts outside VRAM)"),
   so experts outside the cache run on the 15 pool workers at ~80 GB/s DDR5.
   Window anatomy: ~52.12 ms/window = run 48.12 (CUDA0 GPU-reach wait 27.36 +
   CPU experts 20.16) + commit 1.42 + emit 2.58; ~avg 3.35 rows~ per window.
   That is why sharing a layer pass works well here (2 rows ≈ 48 % of the work of
   1 row) *and* why shrinking the cache hurts: the CPU-expert term is the single
   biggest slice of every window.

Readings:
- **MTP drafts are the whole game.** Slots carry no drafts by default; this
  engine's solo speed is draft-dominated (60 with, ~26 without), so plain
  ~--batch~ cost −59 %. With ~--batch-mtp~ the windows do carry proposals
  (~captured the batch window over slots 1,1,0,0~) and the cost collapses to −3 %.
- **~--context~ buys back the cache.** 262144 → 196608 cut slot cost 2.25 → 1.71
  GiB and returned **+2,359 expert slots**, recovering most of the loss.
- ~--kv-grow~ is **auto-off** on this box: ~--kv-grow is off (one GPU, a profile,
  the whole K/V in VRAM, every expert in RAM, no --batch, --vram-elastic or
  --peer-device)~ — printed even with ~--batch~ present, and ~expert_slots~
  unchanged. Lazy K/V is therefore *not* an escape hatch in 0.1.52 SYCL.
- ~--batch~ **without** ~--batch-mtp~ degrades parking, our biggest lever
  (87 % prompt reuse): ~conversation cache: slot 0 not parked before its
  overwrite (its 623 tokens have no per-slot draft K/V without --batch-mtp)~.
  With ~--batch-mtp~ parking from slots works again (~parked 437 tokens from slot 0~).
- Long prompt reads **yield to the slots** once there is a second session:
  ~the prompt read gives way at 73920 of 113651 tokens; slot 1 holds it~,
  ~the prompt was read in 9 parts, the slots decoding 26124 ms between them~.
  That is the cure for the 120 s head-of-line waits, and it works even without
  steady concurrency.

** 3a. What batching does *not* do — read this before promising 4×
BATCHING.md is explicit, and it is the single most important caveat for the
subagent workflow:

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
It does not make independent cold prompts prefill in parallel. The server admits
them one at a time; a running value of four can mean four requests are in
flight, not four slots decoding together. A slot already decoding may make
progress while the next prompt is read, but long independent prompts can finish
before enough admissions overlap to fill every configured slot.
#+END_QUOTE

So four scout lanes do not prefill four prompts at once. What batching *does*
provide, in the doc's words and as observed in our engine log:

- ~Each admission reads the request's prompt through the usual prompt path …
  the slots decode between the prompt's chunks: after each chunk (~--prefill~,
  2048–8192 tokens) they decode for half as long as the chunk took
  (~STRATA_BATCH_DECODE_SHARE~, default 0.5), so a long prompt slows the others
  down instead of stopping them.~ Confirmed live: ~the prompt read gives way at
  73920 of 113651 tokens; slot 1 holds it~, ~the prompt was read in 9 parts,
  the slots decoding 26124 ms between them~.
- ~A long prompt gives way to a short one (#656's cooperative preemption): when
  a request with a prompt under half as long is waiting, the server sends
  BYIELD.~ Orchestrator (65–146 k) beside a scout (2–8 k) is that pattern
  exactly — the new workflow makes yielding worth far more than it was under
  the old traffic.
- ~A request left alone in a slot … goes back to the solo path … (at most twice
  per request; with --prompt-cache 0 it stays in the slot; STRATA_PARALLEL_SOLO=0
  turns it off).~ And the doc's measurement of a draft layer built for another
  conversation accepting as many drafts (140 of 172) as one that read the
  conversation (140 of 173) means the solo-return path keeps its drafts.
- ~More requests than slots wait for a free one (~live.slots~, ~live.running~)~
  — fan out to ~--parallel − 1~ lanes if the orchestrator also decodes, or the
  queue comes back.

The honest expectation: the gain is **decode overlap plus prompt-read yielding**,
not parallel prefill. Warm agent turns read only their new 2–8 k tokens, so
aggregate rises because decodes overlap; the ~first~ turn of each lane still
pays a serial cold read (cold reads measured ~3.4 k tok/s~, so four lanes' first
turns cost ~4 × 8 k tokens ≈ 9 s of admission time, amortised over each run).

Constraints worth writing down, because they bite:
- ~reserveTokens ≥ 32768~ always. The model entry's ~maxTokens 16384~ plus
  ~reasoning_budget_tokens 8192~ means one turn can legitimately want ~25 k~ of
  room; a smaller reserve makes compaction fight the requests.
- ~models.json contextWindow~ must never exceed the engine ~--context~: the cap
  is hard, and an over-cap prompt is a 400, not a graceful shrink.
- ~maxTokens 16384~ on a 98k window is fine, and ~--mtp-max-t~ should be left
  alone: help says ~cap the MTP's windows at M tokens (0 = --spec; longer ones
  come from suffixes)~, and the live engine reports ~spec 4, mtp_max 2~ — the
  draft window is already capped at the ~--spec 2~ the wrapper passes, so the
  budget clamp this knob addresses does not apply to us. Caveat worth a probe:
  ~--spec~ is **absent from 0.1.52's SYCL ~--help~** while ~/metrics~ reports
  ~spec 4~ — the wrapper's ~--spec 2~ is either a no-op or overridden by the
  server, and if it is a no-op then ~--spec 4~ is already live and the draft
  window question is settled by the engine, not by us.
- Other tenants must be brought down with the cap, or they will 400:
  SillyTavern's context slider on ~agents:8001~, and any client that asks for
  262k. rules-lawyer's prompts (4–17 k) are unaffected.

* 6. Required code change (~peteches/services/strata.scm~) — done 2026-11-06
**As implemented it differs from the sketch below**, because ~serve --help~ on
0.1.57 shows ~--parallel~ is a *serve* flag ("requested concurrent engine slots")
while ~--batch N~ / ~--slots N~ are engine flags: the implementation keeps them as
separate config fields (~parallel~ spliced onto the exec line, ~batch~ spliced into
~"args"~, ~batch-mtp?~ defaulting true, plus ~serve-args~ for serve-only flags and
~engine-flag-line~ for valueless ones). Measured outcome in §11.

The catalog path honours ~parallel~ and ~extra-args~:

#+CAPTION: ~strata-launch-args~, lines 401–414 — used only at line 451 (catalog)
#+BEGIN_SRC scheme
(append (list "launch" …)
        (if (strata-configuration-parallel config)
            (list "--parallel" (number->string …)) '())
        (strata-configuration-extra-args config))
#+END_SRC

~comfyui~ runs the **native-pack** path, whose ~--serve~ line is emitted by
~strata-native-wrapper-script~ (line 544 ff.) from a literal arg list that
includes ~--max-context~, ~--kv~, ~--vision~ and the parking flags but **not**
~--parallel~ and **not** ~extra-args~ — verified against the live
~/var/lib/strata/run-strata.sh~ before the patch (0 occurrences of ~--batch~ /
~--parallel~, despite the record having both fields).

There is a **shorter route for the MTP half** that needs no arg patch: the
native wrapper already writes ~export STRATA_SSD_GBPS=…~ lines, and the engine
takes ~STRATA_BATCH_MTP=1~ from the environment (help: "~(STRATA_BATCH_MTP=1
does the same)~"), alongside ~STRATA_KV_GROW~, ~STRATA_PREFILL_AUTO_MAX~,
~STRATA_HOST_CORE~, ~STRATA_POOL_CORES~, ~STRATA_SPEC_COUPLED~; BATCHING.md
adds ~STRATA_BATCH_DECODE_SHARE~ (default 0.5), ~STRATA_PARALLEL_SOLO~,
~STRATA_BATCH_MTP_SERIAL~ and ~STRATA_BATCH_SELF_COMMIT~. An ~env-extra~ field
(or reusing the ~ssd-gbps~ pattern) flips those *today*. Only the lane count
itself needs the arg patch — ~--batch N~ / ~--slots N~ has no env equivalent.

Fix, in the idiom the file already uses for ~reasoning-json~ and
~conv-cache-json~ (precompute at gexp-elaboration time, splice with ~#$~ — a
gexp does not capture let-bound locals, and the file's own comment records the
0-byte ~run-strata.sh~ that mistake produced):

#+BEGIN_SRC scheme
        ;; Batch lanes as "args" lines, precomputed HERE like conv-cache-json.
        ;; The engine's own default for --batch-groups on SYCL/Metal is absent
        ;; (pipeline.* is CUDA/HIP-only), so only the lane count + --batch-mtp are
        ;; emitted; --batch-groups/--pipeline-windows stay catalog-path only.
        (batch-json
         (string-join
          (append
           (if (strata-configuration-parallel config)
               ;; --batch is the documented name for the lane count (help:
               ;; "--batch N / --slots N"); --parallel is the alias the
               ;; catalog path uses.
               (list (string-append "    \"--batch\", \""
                                    (number->string
                                     (strata-configuration-parallel config))
                                    "\",\n"))
               '())
           (if (and (strata-configuration-parallel config)
                    (strata-configuration-batch-mtp? config)
                    ;; MTP is model-scoped, not config-scoped: the wrapper
                    ;; already lets (mtp-dir (strata-native-model-mtp-dir
                    ;; model)) at line 627 — reuse it, and note --spec is
                    ;; emitted as a literal "--spec", "2" at line 766, so
                    ;; --batch-mtp's precondition holds and raising --spec
                    ;; is also a wrapper edit.
                    (strata-native-model-mtp-dir model))
               (list "    \"--batch-mtp\",\n") '())
           (map (lambda (a) (string-append "    \"" (string-quote a) "\",\n"))
                (strata-configuration-extra-args config)))
          ""))
#+END_SRC

and splice ~#$batch-json~ into the ~"args": [ … ]~ block anywhere after the
~--spec~ / ~--mtp~ lines (the measurement harness put it right after the
~"--mmap-experts"~ line, which is inside ~args~ and worked). Add the record field:

#+BEGIN_SRC scheme

             (default #t)

* 6a. Phase 0 — free levers first (no VRAM, no context, no lane cost)
Before paying for lanes with context, measure these: they cost a restart and
nothing else, and single-stream speed is still ~96 %~ of this box's time.

| Lever                                             | Why it is free here |
|---------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| ~--lookup-chain 2~                                 | opt-in: "after the MTP's drafts, add up to K prompt-lookup drafts that continue them (the window grows to at most 8; default 0 = off)". Agent loops repeat tool-call boilerplate, which is what prompt lookup drafts from, and a wider window **amortises the CPU-expert term** that costs 20–34 ms of every window on this card. No VRAM. |
| ~--suffix-draft~ (default 3)                       | already active (that is the ~lookup: 3~ in ~/metrics~); tune it with ~--lookup-chain~. |
| ~--short-read 256~                                 | "read at most N fresh text tokens through the decode windows instead of the batched prompt path (default 64, 0 = off)" — an agent turn's new content (a tool result) is exactly that shape. But ~N~ tokens read at ~60 tok/s~ is ~N/60~ s (256 ≈ 4 s), so this only wins if the prompt-path stall dominates. Measure it, don't assume it. |
| ~--prompt-cache~ (default 6, "~118 MB of RAM each~"), ~--prompt-cache-every 16384~, ~--prompt-cache-tail~ | the ~GEN → BGEN~ slot-handoff checkpoints BATCHING.md describes; ~--prompt-cache 0~ makes a returning conversation re-read its prompt, which is the worst thing that can happen to a 65 k orchestrator turn. RAM, not VRAM. |
| ~--prefill auto:16384~ / ~STRATA_PREFILL_AUTO_MAX~ | bigger chunks = fewer yields = faster cold reads, but each lane stop gets longer. Tune *after* lanes exist. |
| ~--adapt-async 1~                                  | opt-in with the resident-RAM mode (ours: ~R4 hit path ON - resident experts are computed on the GPU~): the adaptive tier's expert swaps advance between verify windows instead of a window waiting for a whole swap. Unmeasured here — worth one probe. |

~--lookup-chain 2~ is the one to run first: it attacks the same CPU-expert-bound
window that caps per-lane speed, for zero VRAM.

* 8. Phase B/C — the target for the subagent workflow
Engine (Phase C):
#+BEGIN_SRC scheme
(context 98304) (parallel 4) (batch-mtp? #t)
(conversation-cache-mib 8192)          ; was 4096 — 4-6 lanes × ~240 MiB windows
(conversation-cache-slots 9)           ; was 5;  evictions=167 already at 5 slots
(extra-args '("--lookup-chain" "2"))   ; wider draft windows, no VRAM
#+END_SRC
Why the parking numbers move: parked conversations live in **RAM** (measured
~pinned_mib 3,875~, each park ~30–60 ms~ and a ~24576-token/~240 MiB window), and
evictions today (167) already cost full prompt re-reads. Under fan-out every lane
is its own conversation, so 5 park slots is not enough — an evicted orchestrator
turn is a ~65 k-token re-read (~15–20 s).

pi (Phase C): ~contextWindow 98304~, ~reserveTokens 32768~, ~keepRecentTokens 32768~.

Lane arithmetic: ~--parallel 4~ means the orchestrator **plus three** subagents —
the orchestrator's own decode holds a slot while it waits on its children. Four
scouts alongside it need ~--parallel 5~ = 4.6 GiB of session K/V rather than
3.4 GiB at ~98304~, i.e. roughly ~800~ expert slots dearer. Read the startup
log's ~expert cache N slots~ line before promising five lanes; the doc's rule is
that requests beyond the slot count simply wait, so fan-out past ~--parallel~
moves the queue rather than removing it.

Acceptance thresholds (run the same harness used for §3 —
~/tmp/arm.py~ + ~/tmp/patch_wrapper.py~, driven over
~ssh -T comfyui.ts 'sudo -n python3 -'~):

| Check                             | Pass condition                              |
|-----------------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| ~expert_slots~ after start         | ≥ 13,000 (≥ 55 % residency)                 |
| ~batch_slots~ / ~concurrency.serving~ | == ~--parallel~                          |
| ~GET /metrics~ → ~live.slots~, ~live.running~ during a 4-lane probe | ≥ 2 slots in state ~decoding~ at once. The doc's own warning: "a running value of four can mean four requests are in flight, not four slots decoding together" — serial admission with one busy slot is a *fail*, not a pass. |
| doc-prescribed handoff test       | long request alone, then a second one while it decodes; the first's ~GEN → BGEN~ transfer must report "a large reused prefix and only the new token(s) read" (BATCHING.md §"After changing the setting…") |
| sequential ×4 (one lane)         | ≥ 52 tok/s (within 15 % of the 60 ceiling)  |
| 4 concurrent ×4                  | ≥ 110 tok/s aggregate (~2× ceiling)         |
| TTFT p95 at 4 open connections   | < 3 s                                       |
| ~conversation_cache evictions~    | not rising vs. baseline rate                |
| production                        | no 400s from clients pinned at 262k         |

If "sequential ×4" fails, the context is too small / cache residency too low:
step back to Phase B (131072 / 4 lanes) or A.

* 11. Phase C — the plumbing, as implemented (deployed 2026-11-06)
§6's gap is closed. Three fields were added to ~<strata-configuration>~
(~batch~, ~batch-mtp?~, ~serve-args~), ~parallel~ and ~extra-args~ are now spliced
into the native wrapper, and ~engine-flag-line~ exists for valueless flags. All
of it is precomputed in the wrapper's ~let*~ and interpolated with ~#$~, which is
the only way a gexp can see it (the 0-byte ~run-strata.sh~ trap noted in
~peteches/services/strata.scm~).

** What ~--parallel~ actually costs here — measured, and the reason it is off
Requesting lanes does work (with ~(parallel 5)~ ~"concurrency": {"serving": 5,
"requested": 5}" appears in ~(/v1/status)~), but each requested slot is a batch
window holding its own session K/V, and on this card those windows are paid for
out of the profile-filled expert cache:

| (parallel)      | expert slots | expert cache | solo   | 2 conc | 4 conc | TTFT at 4 |
|-----------------+--------------+--------------+--------+--------+--------+-----------|
| off (deployed)  | 17,830       | 24,480 MiB   | 64.8   | 64.4   | 64.5   | 0.87 s    |
| ~--parallel 5"  | 9,432        | 12,973 MiB   | 49.6   | 42.0   | 38.7   | 13.8 s    |

So lanes here *cost* throughput rather than buying it: after Phase U the engine
already serves 2 and 4 concurrent requests at essentially the single-stream rate
with no slots requested at all. ~--kv-grow~ is the documented countermeasure (and
measured +34 % in §3's batch arms), but it has not been re-validated on this
configuration without ~--batch~, so it is not enabled either. If a batch arm is
ever wanted it is one word in ~peteches/systems/comfyui.scm~ — and ~batch-mtp?~
defaults true, because ~--batch~ without it measured −59 %.

** Two constraints learned by breaking the service
1. ~--queue-timeout~ on its own makes ~strata serve~ refuse to start:
   "queue_timeout requires max_queue to bound pending calls". It is configured
   as a pair — ~'("--max-queue" "12" "--queue-timeout" "300")~ — 12 being a full
   pi fan-out (orchestrator plus four sub-agents) plus one or two tenant turns,
   and 300 s being comfortably above the ~84 s" a 113k-token orchestrator prompt
   read takes at the measured 1350 tok/s.
2. Deploy regenerates ~run-strata.sh~ but does not restart the service — the
   known shepherd-reload gap in ~docs/fleet-deployment.org~. The new flags only
   take effect after ~herd restart strata~, so verify the live exec line, not the
   file, when checking whether a change landed.

** Parking: 5 windows → 9\nNine parked windows, one per lane, inside the same
4096 MiB budget (a parked window measured ~240 MiB), so this is not extra RAM;
the earlier 5 slots evicted (167 evictions in the log before the change).

** pi side: there is no lane cap to set\nGrepped the installed
~pi-interactive-subagents~ extension on agents: its environment names are
~PI_SUBAGENT_{ACTIVITY_FILE,AGENT,ALLOWED,AUTO_EXIT,HERDR_PARENT_RATIO,ID,MUX,
NAME,SESSION,SHELL_READY_DELAY_MS,SURFACE}~ — no ~maxParallel~, no concurrency
limit. So the number of lanes that actually arrive is set by how the
orchestrator spawns, and the engine side is the queue bound above. ~reserveTokens
98304~ in ~(~/.pi/agent/settings.json)~ stays as it is; the engine keeps its full
~262144~ context because concurrency is free without slots.
